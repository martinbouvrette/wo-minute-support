Born in the field

When waiting gets too much, you need an anchor.

Some moments call for something to hold on to: a transition between two activities, an emotion on the rise, a wait that drags on. A painting that moves gently gives the eyes support and the body time to settle.

No numbers rushing you, no screen shouting: a small world that moves along, and shows that it's moving along.

Why a painting

The same time, three ways.

Twenty seconds, shown three times at once. Start them, and notice which one you follow.

0:20

A number

A number. You need to read the clock, and count down in your head. For many people, “0:12” means nothing.

A red disc

A red disc. You see what's left without reading the clock: it's a staple of many classrooms. But there's nothing else to look at — the wait is still a wait.

A painting

A painting. You see that it's moving along, and there's something to follow in the meantime. When the lighthouse lights up, it's over.

Time you can watch

Sixteen living paintings, computed live.

Each painting is a small story that unfolds during the countdown — never a looping video. The sun sets over the sea, a shower fills its water clock, a candle burns down, the snow builds a snowman, a canvas is laid down tile by tile, a city is filmed at night — by helicopter, by boat, by subway, by taxi —, a robot transforms into sixteen things. A cherry tree drops one petal a second onto a pond, a mouse looks for its cheese in a maze and sometimes gives us a curious look, a book writes itself word by word, star-shaped ink drops blend under a brush. A little train carries its passengers to the end of the line, a vegetable garden grows from seed to harvest. The longer the timer, the more happens.

We don't name them: we watch them.

Real footage of the app, filmed on iPad. Hover over a painting: it wakes up.

    For those who can read

    A book that writes itself while you wait.

    Six little illustrated stories write themselves word by word, in big letters; the incoming word is highlighted, and each important word brings its picture to life — the cat appears when you read “cat”. With sound on, a voice reads each sentence. A story roughly every two minutes, each with its own ending.

    In English and French, following the device language.

    The music

    Every painting has its own music.

    Composed live on the device, in step with the painting: the surf under the sailboat, the lullaby of the candlelit night, raindrops that shift from minor to major as the color comes back, a Japanese scale under the cherry tree, a harp note for each word of the book, the little train's whistle at every departure, a drop for each sprout in the garden. A fanfare when the robot transforms, another when the mouse finds its cheese.

    It plays even in silent mode: turn it down, or off in Options.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does the timer go off when the screen is off?

    Yes, if notifications are allowed. Wo Minute asks the first time you start a timer; you can also turn them on from the menu, under “Our commitments”. If you said no: Settings ▸ Wo Minute ▸ Notifications.

    Is there sound?

    Two things. Every painting has its own music, composed live; it plays even in silent mode, and can be turned off in Options. At the end, an alert of your choice: a sound, a vibration or a spoken announcement.

    How long can a timer last?

    From 1 to 12 minutes, one minute per notch of the ring. The longer it is, the more happens in the painting.

    How many paintings are there?

    Sixteen: the ink drop, the sailboat, the cherry tree, the candle, the maze, the rain, the balls, the book, the stars, the spiral, the snowflakes, the studio, the city, the robot, the little train and the vegetable garden. Each one changes from one session to the next.

    Why don't the paintings have names?

    We don't name them: we watch them. For those who can't see the screen, VoiceOver says the name of each painting.

    The balls fall too fast to have time to name them.

    In Options, the falling speed can be slowed down. One still falls every second; tap one, and a voice says its name.

    Where did the chained phases go?

    Version 2.0 removed them: one painting, one length. To chain two moments, start a second painting when the first one ends.

    Is the app in English?

    Yes, since 2.0: it follows the device language. This site also has a French page.

    Does it work offline?

    Entirely. No connection is required, no data is sent.
